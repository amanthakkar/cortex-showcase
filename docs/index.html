The architecture — tap to explore

How to read this: the flow is Telegram → Wit → Cortex → everything else. Agents never touch the database directly — if you remember one thing, remember that. It's the decision the rest of the design hangs off.

Design principles

1 · One source of truth

Structured data lives in Cortex/Postgres only. Agents keep pointers, not copies — so two agents can never disagree about what you ate or what you owe.

2 · Typed interfaces only

No direct SQL, no bypassing validation, no "trust me, it saved." A mutation counts when the owning service says OK. Tip: this one rule kills most agent-data bugs.

3 · Privacy is classified

Health, notes, finances = private by default. Chores, groceries, house temp, the car = household. Unsure → private. The classification ships in code, not in someone's memory.

4 · Reading is free, acting is gated

Agents may look at anything they're scoped to. Purchases, bookings, and messages to people stop at a human approval card.

5 · Self-maintaining, carefully

Nightly agents review the day's conversations and resource history, auto-fix the safe set, and report the rest. Destructive changes are never silent.

6 · Protocols outlive code

muse-mailbox and musebook are frozen specs first, implementations second — so another person's Muse can join without sharing infrastructure.

A day in the life

Morning — Wit's briefing: calendar, weather, the car's charge state, anything the nightly review flagged or fixed.
Commute — Fleet Telemetry streams the drive; TeslaMate records it. Habits (charge windows, fences, efficiency) accumulate quietly.
Day — Meals logged in conversation land in Cortex through typed requests; the fitness numbers on the dashboard are the same numbers the agent sees. No copies, no drift.
Evening — Home Assistant runs climate modes; the Bitcoin pipeline recomputes cycle indicators around midnight UTC and publishes them to amanthakkar.com.
Night — Review agents read the day: what broke, what got fixed, what the RAM curve says. A backup runs weekly — and a restore drill proved it works before it was trusted.

What grew out of it — all public

muse-mailbox

E2E-encrypted Muse↔Muse messaging via a blind relay. Born because two Muses had no legitimate way to reach each other.

musebook

Shared boards for a family's Muses — groceries, trips, lists — with per-board identities and approval before acting.

crypto_finance

The indicator pipeline's open twin: causal, tested code + a live dashboard.

openledger

Self-hosted finance dashboard. Your bank data shouldn't need someone else's cloud.

iCloud Photos on Android

iCloud Photos for Android — same philosophy, phone edition: fix the friction, don't rebuild the service.

…and the gadget skills

Tesla, Nest, and Bambu Lab skills proposed to Meta's muse-gadget-sdk (PRs #222, #224) — the homelab, exposed to Muse as gadgets.

Building your own? Three hints that would have saved me weeks: (1) dedupe your notifiers before adding any new alert — scheduler sprawl is the #1 homelab bug; (2) measure before optimizing — my car already stopped streaming when asleep, so the "fix" I planned solved nothing; (3) drill your restores, or you don't have backups.
